About

Fiscal federalism, in practice

I am a Ph.D. candidate in public affairs at Indiana University’s O’Neill School, where my research sits at the intersection of public budgeting, health care finance, and fiscal federalism. I am interested in a deceptively simple question: when the federal government changes the price of a dollar, how do state and local governments respond?

My job market paper takes up that question through Medicaid provider taxes — a financing tool that lets states leverage federal matching funds. Related work examines the fiscal footprint of federal deduction caps, the budgetary effects of short‑term rental platforms on local governments, and labor cost dynamics in the nursing home industry.

Alongside my research I teach undergraduate public budgeting and finance at the O’Neill School, where I work to make fiscal policy legible — and useful — to students headed into public service.
